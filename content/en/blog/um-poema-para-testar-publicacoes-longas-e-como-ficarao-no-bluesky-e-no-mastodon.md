I am nothing.
I shall never be anything.
I cannot wish to be anything.
Apart from that, I have within me all the dreams in the world.

Windows of my room,
Of my room in one of the world’s millions of houses that no one knows who lives in
(And if they knew who lived there, what would they know?),
You open onto the mystery of a street constantly crossed by people,
Onto a street inaccessible to every thought,
Real, impossibly real, certain, unknowably certain,
With the mystery of things beneath the stones and living beings,
With death putting dampness on the walls and white hair on men,
With Destiny driving the cart of everything down the road of nothing.

Today I am defeated, as if I knew the truth.
Today I am lucid, as if I were about to die,
And had no fellowship left with things
Except a farewell, with this house and this side of the street becoming
A row of carriages in a train, and a whistled departure
From inside my head,
And a jolt of my nerves and a creaking of bones as it leaves.

Today I am perplexed, like someone who thought and found and forgot.
Today I am divided between the loyalty I owe
To the Tobacco Shop across the street, as something real on the outside,
And the feeling that everything is a dream, as something real on the inside.

I have failed at everything.
Since I had no purpose at all, perhaps everything was nothing.
The education they gave me,
I climbed down from it through the back window of the house,
I went out into the country with great ambitions.
But there I found only grass and trees,
And whenever there were people, they were just like the others.
I step away from the window, sit down in a chair. What am I to think about?

What do I know of what I shall be, I who do not know what I am?
To be what I think? But I think I am so many things!
And so many people think they are the same thing that there cannot be that many!
A genius? At this very moment
A hundred thousand minds dream themselves geniuses like me,
And history, who knows, will not remember even one,
And nothing will remain but manure from so many future conquests.
No, I do not believe in myself.
Every madhouse has lunatics mad with so many certainties!
Am I, who have no certainty at all, more right or less right?
No, not even in myself…
In how many attics and non-attics throughout the world
Are self-styled geniuses dreaming at this very hour?
How many lofty and noble and lucid aspirations—
Yes, truly lofty and noble and lucid—
And who knows whether achievable,
Will never see the light of the real sun or find a human ear?
The world belongs to those born to conquer it,
Not to those who dream they can conquer it, even if they are right.
I have dreamed more than Napoleon ever accomplished.
I have clasped more of humanity to my hypothetical breast than Christ,
I have secretly devised philosophies no Kant ever wrote.
But I am, and perhaps shall always be, the man in the attic,
Even if I do not live in one;
I shall always be the man who was not born for it;
I shall always be merely the man who had potential;
I shall always be the man who waited for a door to be opened to him beside a wall with no door,
And sang the song of the Infinite in a chicken coop,
And heard the voice of God in a sealed-up well.
Believe in myself? No, nor in anything.
Let Nature pour over my burning head
Her sun, her rain, the wind that finds my hair,
And let the rest come if it comes, or has to come, or not come at all.
Heart-bound slaves of the stars,
We conquer the whole world before getting out of bed;
But we wake up and it is opaque,
We get up and it is alien,
We leave the house and it is the entire earth,
Plus the solar system and the Milky Way and the Indefinite.

(Eat chocolates, little girl;
Eat chocolates!
Look, there is no metaphysics in the world but chocolates.
Look, all religions teach no more than the confectioner’s art.
Eat, dirty little girl, eat!
If only I could eat chocolates with the same truth with which you eat them!
But I think, and as I remove the silver paper, which is tinfoil,
I throw everything on the floor, as I have thrown away my life.)

But at least, from the bitterness of what I shall never be,
There remains the swift handwriting of these verses,
A broken portico leading to the Impossible.
But at least I dedicate to myself a tearless contempt,
Noble at least in the sweeping gesture with which I cast
The dirty laundry that I am, without a list, into the flow of things,
And stay at home without a shirt.

(You who console me, who do not exist and therefore console me,
Or Greek goddess, conceived as a statue come to life,
Or Roman patrician woman, impossibly noble and ill-fated,
Or princess of troubadours, most gracious and colorful,
Or eighteenth-century marquise, low-cut and remote,
Or famous courtesan from our parents’ time,
Or some modern something—I cannot quite imagine what—
All of this, whatever it may be, whatever you may be, if it can inspire me, let it inspire me!
My heart is an emptied bucket.
As those who summon spirits summon spirits, I summon
Myself and find nothing.
I go to the window and see the street with absolute clarity.
I see the shops, I see the sidewalks, I see the passing cars,
I see the clothed living beings crossing paths,
I see the dogs that also exist,
And all of this weighs upon me like a sentence of exile,
And all of this is foreign, like everything.)

I lived, studied, loved, and even believed,
And today there is no beggar I do not envy simply because he is not me.
I look at each one’s rags and sores and lies,
And think: perhaps you never lived or studied or loved or believed
(Because it is possible to create the reality of all that without doing any of it);
Perhaps you merely existed, like a lizard whose tail has been cut off
And whose tail is still wriggling, apart from the lizard.

I made of myself what I did not know how to make,
And what I could have made of myself, I did not.
The costume I wore was wrong.
They immediately took me for someone I was not, and I did not correct them, and I lost myself.
When I tried to remove the mask,
It was stuck to my face.
When I took it off and looked at myself in the mirror,
I had already grown old.
I was drunk; I no longer knew how to put on the costume I had never taken off.
I threw away the mask and slept in the dressing room
Like a dog tolerated by the management
Because it is harmless,
And I am going to write this story to prove that I am sublime.

Musical essence of my useless verses,
If only I could find you as something I had made,
And not remain forever facing the Tobacco Shop across the street,
Trampling underfoot the awareness that I exist,
Like a rug a drunkard trips over
Or a doormat stolen by gypsies that was worth nothing.

But the Owner of the Tobacco Shop came to the door and stood there.
I looked at him with the discomfort of my head turned awkwardly
And the discomfort of my soul misunderstanding.
He will die and I will die.
He will leave behind the signboard, and I will leave behind verses.
At some point the signboard will die too, and the verses as well.
After a while the street where the signboard hung will die,
And so will the language in which the verses were written.
Then the spinning planet on which all this took place will die.
On other satellites of other systems, something like people
Will go on making things like verses and living beneath things like signboards,
Always one thing facing the other,
Always one thing as useless as the other,
Always the impossible as stupid as the real,
Always the mystery of the depths as certain as the mysterious sleep of the surface,
Always this, or always something else, or neither one thing nor the other.

But a man entered the Tobacco Shop (to buy tobacco?),
And plausible reality suddenly falls upon me.
I half rise, energetic, convinced, human,
And I intend to write these verses in which I say the opposite.

I light a cigarette at the thought of writing them
And savor in the cigarette my liberation from all thoughts.
I follow the smoke as if it had a path of its own,
And enjoy, in a sensitive and fitting moment,
My liberation from all speculation
And the awareness that metaphysics is the consequence of feeling unwell.

Then I lean back in the chair
And keep smoking.
As long as Destiny allows it, I shall keep smoking.

(If I married my washerwoman’s daughter,
Perhaps I would be happy.)
That being so, I rise from the chair. I go to the window.

The man left the Tobacco Shop (putting change into his trouser pocket?).
Ah, I know him: it is Esteves, without metaphysics.
(The Owner of the Tobacco Shop came to the door.)
As if by divine instinct, Esteves turned around and saw me.
He waved goodbye, I shouted, Goodbye, Esteves!, and the universe
Reassembled itself for me without ideal or hope, and the Owner of the Tobacco Shop smiled.
